# Reykjavik, Iceland

Reykjavik is small enough to walk across in an afternoon, but it's really
the launch point for the rest of Iceland rather than a destination in
itself. Most visitors spend a day or two in the city and the rest of the
trip driving the ring road past waterfalls, glaciers, and black sand
beaches.

The entire country runs on geothermal energy, and hot springs are a daily
ritual here rather than a tourist novelty. The Blue Lagoon is the famous
one, but smaller, less crowded geothermal pools are scattered across the
countryside and often cheaper. Locals tend to prefer their neighborhood
public pool over any tourist spot.

Winter brings the northern lights, best seen well outside the city's light
pollution on a clear, cold night, along with only four or five hours of
daylight. Summer flips completely, with nearly twenty-four hours of light
and a much milder, still-unpredictable climate.

Iceland is one of the more expensive countries to travel in, especially for
food and alcohol, so many visitors save money by cooking in a guesthouse
kitchen rather than eating out every meal. Weather can shift dramatically
within the same day, so layered, waterproof clothing matters more here than
almost anywhere else on this list.
