# New Orleans, Louisiana

New Orleans runs on music the way other cities run on traffic. Brass bands
play on street corners in the French Quarter, and it's genuinely normal for
a bar's lineup to start after midnight. Frenchmen Street, just outside the
main tourist strip, is where a lot of locals go instead of Bourbon Street.

The food culture blends French, Spanish, African, and Caribbean influences
into something that doesn't really exist anywhere else. Gumbo, jambalaya,
and po'boys are everywhere, but a beignet from Cafe du Monde, dusted heavily
in powdered sugar, is the one thing almost every visitor ends up trying.

The city's cemeteries are unusual by American standards: tombs are built
above ground because the water table sits too high for traditional burial.
St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 is the oldest and most visited, though several
require a guided tour to enter.

Summers are brutally hot and humid, with afternoon thunderstorms almost
guaranteed. Hurricane season runs from June through November and can
seriously disrupt travel plans with little warning. Mardi Gras, in February
or March depending on the year, is the single most crowded and most
expensive week to visit.
