# Denver, Colorado

Denver sits at the edge of the Rocky Mountains, a mile above sea level, which
locals will mention within about five minutes of meeting you. The city itself
is walkable and easygoing, but its real draw is how fast you can leave it
behind. Within an hour you can be standing on a mountain trail with the
skyline nowhere in sight.

Rocky Mountain National Park is the big-ticket destination, with alpine lakes,
elk herds, and trails that climb well above the treeline. Closer to downtown,
Red Rocks Amphitheatre hosts concerts inside a natural sandstone formation,
and Cherry Creek offers an easy paved path for a slower afternoon outdoors.

The city has one of the highest concentrations of craft breweries per capita
in the country, and brewery patios are a genuine social hub here, not just a
tourist stop. Food trucks cluster around Civic Center Park most weekdays,
serving everything from green chile to Ethiopian food.

Denver's dry climate means intense sun year-round and cold, clear winter
nights good for stargazing. Summer afternoons often bring sudden, short
thunderstorms. Spring can dump a surprising amount of snow with almost no
warning, so layers are a genuinely practical packing choice here, not just
advice.
