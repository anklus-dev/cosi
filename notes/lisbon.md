# Lisbon, Portugal

Lisbon is built across seven hills above the Tagus River, so almost every
walk ends with either a steep climb or a sweeping view, often both.
Colorful tiled building facades, called azulejos, cover entire streets and
turn ordinary apartment blocks into something worth photographing.

The city's old yellow trams, especially the number 28 line, climb routes too
narrow and steep for a normal bus, threading through neighborhoods like
Alfama and Graca. Miradouros, small viewpoint terraces scattered across the
hills, are where locals gather in the evening with a drink to watch the
sunset over the rooftops.

Pastel de nata, a custard tart with a caramelized top, is sold everywhere but
best fresh and warm from a bakery rather than a tourist cafe. Seafood is
cheap and excellent here; grilled sardines are a summer staple, especially
during the Santo Antonio festival in June.

Lisbon is one of the more affordable capital cities in Western Europe, and
its mild winters rarely dip below freezing, making it a popular shoulder
season destination. Summers get hot and dry, and the hills mean comfortable
shoes matter more here than in almost any other European capital.
