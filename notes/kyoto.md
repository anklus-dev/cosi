# Kyoto, Japan

Kyoto was the imperial capital for over a thousand years, and it still moves
at a slower, more deliberate pace than Tokyo. Narrow lanes, wooden machiya
townhouses, and small shrines tucked between buildings give whole
neighborhoods a hushed, unhurried feeling even during the day.

Fushimi Inari's thousands of vermillion torii gates wind up a mountainside
and are famous for good reason, but the crowds thin out dramatically past the
first thirty minutes of climbing. Arashiyama's bamboo grove is quieter early
in the morning, before the tour buses arrive. Kinkaku-ji, the golden
pavilion, reflects directly onto the pond in front of it on calm days.

Kaiseki, a traditional multi-course meal built around seasonal ingredients,
originated in the tea ceremony culture here and is still taken seriously as
an art form rather than just a meal. Nishiki Market is a narrow covered
street of small food stalls, good for grazing rather than a sit-down dinner.

Cherry blossom season in early April and the changing maple leaves in
November are the two most crowded, most photographed, and most expensive
times to visit. Late autumn and midwinter are quieter, colder, and easier
to actually get a seat at a temple garden bench.
