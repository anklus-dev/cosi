# Singapore

Singapore is a small, densely built city-state where efficient public
transit, strict cleanliness laws, and dense tropical greenery all coexist
within the same few blocks. The MRT subway system is fast, cheap, and
air-conditioned, which matters given the year-round heat and humidity.

Hawker centers are the heart of the food scene: open-air complexes packed
with independent stalls, many family-run for generations, serving dishes
like chicken rice, laksa, and chili crab for a fraction of restaurant
prices. Maxwell Food Centre and Lau Pa Sat are two of the best-known.

Gardens by the Bay's Supertree Grove and the Cloud Forest dome show off the
city's investment in blending architecture with plant life. Sentosa Island,
connected by cable car, has beaches and theme parks for a more conventional
tourist day.

The climate is consistently hot and humid all year, with short, heavy
afternoon downpours that pass quickly rather than settling in for the day.
Singapore is also known for strict laws around littering, chewing gum, and
jaywalking, enforced with real fines rather than as an empty warning.
